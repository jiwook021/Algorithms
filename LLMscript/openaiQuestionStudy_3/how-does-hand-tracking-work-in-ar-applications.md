Imagine you're playing a game where you have to catch virtual butterflies with your hands.  How does the app know where your hands are in real-time? That's hand tracking.  Here's how it works in simple steps:

**1. The Camera Sees You:** Your phone or device's camera constantly takes pictures (at a very high speed).

**2. Image Processing:**  A powerful computer program (an algorithm) inside the device analyzes each picture looking for specific things:

   * **Finding shapes:** It looks for skin tones, the general shape of hands (fingers, palm), and how those shapes move from one picture to the next.  It's like tracing the outline of your hands in each frame.

   * **Depth Perception:**  Many hand tracking systems use depth sensors (like those in newer phones) or clever tricks with a regular camera to understand how far away different parts of your hand are.  This is how it knows your hand isn't flat against the screen, but actually has volume.

   * **Filtering Out Noise:** The algorithm has to ignore other things in the image (like your clothing, the background, or even other skin tones). This is crucial because the computer needs to focus only on your hands.

**3. 3D Model Creation:** Once the program has located your hands, it creates a 3D model of them in virtual space. This model updates constantly, following every tiny movement.

**4. Interaction with the AR World:**  Finally, the app uses the 3D model of your hands to let you interact with the augmented reality environment.  If you close your hand, the virtual butterfly is caught. If you point, something might happen in the game.

**In short:**  The hand tracking system is like a super-fast, sophisticated video game that watches your hands, figures out their position and movement, and then uses this information to make things happen in the virtual world.  It's all about clever image processing and understanding shapes and movements.
